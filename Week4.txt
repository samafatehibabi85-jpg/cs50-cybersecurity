5. Tor sends your internet traffic through three random volunteer computers (nodes) around the world. It wraps your data in layers of encryption, like an onion. The first node knows who you are but not where you are going, the last node knows where you are going but not who you are, and the middle node knows neither. This makes it impossible for anyone watching to track both your identity and the websites you visit.
6. Just like 1-800-FLOWERS is much easier for a human to remember than the actual 10-digit phone number, domain names (like google.com) are easy-to-remember names that map to complex IP addresses that computers use to find each other.

9. A sandbox isolates the browser from the rest of your computer. If a hacker or malware takes over a tab, they are trapped inside the sandbox and can't access your personal files or steal your data from other parts of your system.
